# How to Run a Competitive Analysis for a Local-Market Real Estate Broker (When Personal-Brand Visibility Is Attracting the Wrong Inquiries)

Most real estate brokers in a metro market don't have a *traffic* problem — they have an *intent* problem. The follower count is fine; the inquiries are not. People are commenting, DM'ing, and reacting, but the messages are not from buyers with budget. They're personal, conversational, or off-topic.

When a broker hits this wall, what they actually need is a competitive analysis built around a specific question: *What are the brokers in my market who get qualified-buyer inquiries doing differently from me — and which of those moves can I adopt in 30 days?*

This guide is the framework. It works for any metro real-estate niche and any language market.

## Step 1 — Define what "winning" looks like before naming any competitors

The trap most agencies fall into: they pick the five biggest accounts in the city and analyze them. That's a vanity benchmark. The right competitors are the ones whose *inquiry quality* you want — not whose follower count you want.

Two filters:

- **Same niche specificity.** If the client sells pre-construction high-rise to investors, ignore broad residential agents. The competitor set is "people whose audience would qualify for your client's listings if they switched accounts tomorrow."
- **Demonstrable intent quality.** Look for signals: a WhatsApp link in bio (not a raw phone number), branded inquiry funnels, comment sections full of property questions instead of personal compliments, captions that pose qualifier questions ("¿cuál es tu horizonte de inversión?") instead of personality hooks.

Limit the list to five. Three should be direct peers in the same gender/demographic if the client's positioning challenge is gendered (which it often is). Two should be institutional benchmarks — a brokerage-of-record, a media-led creator — who own playbooks the individual can adapt.

## Step 2 — Audit each competitor on seven authority signals

For each of the five, document:

1. **Bio credential lockup.** Does the first line lead with a credential ("Certified," "Authorized Sales for [Developer]," "+10 años") or with a personality token ("Miss," "Lifestyle," a nickname)? The first line of the bio is the only line most people read.
2. **Tagline pattern.** A repeatable phrase that anchors every post. The strongest taglines are property-coded ("Off-Market & High-End"), zone-coded ("Tu agente en [neighborhood]"), or business-owner-coded ("Empresaria inmobiliaria") — never persona-coded.
3. **Highlight-reel naming.** Branded by *project name* or *zone* signals inventory depth and prior closings. Branded by emotion or event ("Vibes," "Travels," "Mood") signals lifestyle account.
4. **Grid composition.** Estimate the percentage of grid posts where the *property* is the hero subject vs. where the *agent's face* is the hero subject. Top-performing peers run 60–80% property-first.
5. **Third-party validation displayed.** Press placements, government/industry awards, conference appearances, developer co-branding. These can each be pinned in a single highlight; the absence of this highlight is often the missing trust signal.
6. **Lead-capture mechanism.** Raw phone number = open door for all intent. WhatsApp Business with a pre-filled qualifier message, a Calendly link, or a multi-question form = filtered intent.
7. **Content pillars.** Categorize the last 30 posts: Educational / Listings / Testimonios / Behind-the-scenes / Personal-lifestyle. Map percentages. A serious advisor account runs 30–40% educational. A lifestyle-influencer account runs 30–50% personal.

Build a single seven-column matrix with five rows. You will find that the cohort converges on the same signals — and that the client diverges from them in two or three specific places. Those divergences ARE the brief.

## Step 3 — Map the intent-keyword journey for the local market

Skip the global SEO tool report; it's noise for a hyper-local niche. Instead, manually build a three-tier keyword map:

- **Top-of-funnel (TOFU)** — informational. "Best neighborhoods to invest in [city] 2026," "Is preventa a good investment," "How much do I need to start investing in real estate in [city]." These power lead magnets and educational content.
- **Middle-of-funnel (MOFU)** — comparison. "[Zone A] vs [Zone B] for investment," "ROI on apartment in [specific zone]," "[Developer name] reviews." Lower volume, higher intent, lower competition. These power thought-leadership posts and email nurture.
- **Bottom-of-funnel (BOFU)** — transactional. "Buy apartment [zone] [price band]," "Real estate advisor [city]," "Book consultation real estate [city]." Almost no SEO competition for the *advisor-as-landing-page* angle. These power the funnel page itself.

Rank each keyword on three axes: estimated volume (qualitative), SERP saturation, and advisor-angle opportunity (is the SERP dominated by portals like Zillow / Inmuebles24, leaving room for a personal-advisor voice?). The third axis is where you find the wins. Portals will always outrank an individual broker on broad terms — but they won't show up at all for "asesora autorizada [developer] [city]" or "ROI [specific project] [year]."

Pick three keywords as the priority list and hand them to the writer with format guidance.

## Step 4 — Find the content gaps the cohort has left open

Two ways content gaps emerge:

- **Topical gaps.** Read the top-10 published pieces in the niche. Note what they all say (consensus) and what none of them say (counter-narrative). The unowned counter-narrative is usually a *disqualifier* post — every developer-side blog says "yes, buy preventa now" because they sell preventa; nobody publishes "five reasons NOT to buy preventa in [city] this year." A qualifier-first piece from an advisor IS the differentiator.
- **Format gaps.** What format is missing entirely? Often it's process content — escrow walkthroughs, financing-structure breakdowns, on-site inspection videos. Brokers rarely document the *process* of their own job in detail because it feels mundane to them. To the buyer evaluating who to trust with seven-figure transactions, it's gold.

Both kinds of gaps should map back to the keyword tiers above. A gap with no underlying search demand is a vanity project; a gap that maps to a Tier 2 (MOFU) keyword with low competition is a high-ROI publish.

## Step 5 — Translate the hashtag strategy from broad-volume to intent-filter

The hashtag mix most brokers use defaults to high-volume tags (#realestate, #bienesraices, #realtor). These tags surface content to people browsing the *category* — not people with *location plus budget plus timeline*. For a broker with an intent problem, the hashtag strategy is part of the upstream cause.

Restructure into three tiers (8–12 tags total):

- **Tier 1 — Geo-intent (3–4 tags).** Force-qualify location. #InversiónMonterrey2026 or #InvestInMiami2026 or #BuyCondoNYC. Combine the action verb with the city.
- **Tier 2 — Intent-coded (3–4 tags).** Force-qualify *what the user wants*. #PreventaMonterrey or #ROIRealEstate or #FixAndFlipDallas. Verb + niche + market.
- **Tier 3 — Branded / partnership (2–3 tags).** Build long-term brand equity. The client's own branded tag, the developer's co-branded tag, a series-name tag.

Drop the high-volume category tags entirely. They are not helping. The numerical reach goes down — but the inquiry quality goes up, which is the actual goal.

## Step 6 — Translate findings into a 30-day execution brief

A competitive analysis that lives in a slide deck is wasted work. Translate findings into a small, ordered list of specific moves the client can make in the next 30 days. Three categories:

- **Zero-cost moves (week 1).** Bio rewrite. Highlight-reel relabeling. Hashtag mix swap. Pinned-post creation. These cost nothing and can ship in days.
- **Low-cost production moves (weeks 2–3).** Branded graphic template for grid posts. Co-branded developer-relationship card. One reshoot session for professional-context (on-site) headshots. Lead magnet PDF draft.
- **Funnel / system moves (weeks 3–4).** WhatsApp Business with pre-filled qualifier. Booking link. First investor-segmented email capture page. Tracking pixels.

The ordering matters. If the client makes the funnel/system moves first while the bio still leads with the wrong identity, the funnel will be fed bad-intent traffic. The bio and pinned post have to ship first.

## What this framework does NOT do

It does not solve the agency problem of *whether the client should reposition entirely*. If after the audit the client's account is fundamentally a lifestyle-creator account that happens to mention property, the right answer might be: split the account into two. Keep the personal one. Build a new business account from zero with the right visual and verbal identity from day one. A competitive analysis cannot make that decision — only the client and the agency principal can. But the analysis gives both parties the data to make it on something better than gut.

The dating-DM / wrong-intent problem is fixable. It is almost never a traffic problem. It is almost always a *signal* problem — the signals the account is sending don't match the signals the audience the client wants is scanning for. Tighten the signals, and the inquiries that arrive will start matching the inquiries the client wants.
